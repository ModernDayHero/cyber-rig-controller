How to play

  1. Press Start debugging.
  2. BUG.EXE flies after the Octlets, tentacles first.
  3. Click or tap the bug's body. Each hit flips one bit from 0 to 1, then the bug glitches away to somewhere new.
  4. Watch for the flicker where it reappears and hit it again.
  5. Flip all 8 bits before it eats all 6 Octlets. When you win, every Octlet it ate comes back.

Tip: the bug can't be hit while it's still flickering in. Wait until it's solid.

The science

What is a byte?

Each Octlet is a byte. Computers store everything as numbers, and a byte holds one number from 0 to 255. Every byte is made of 8 bits, and each bit is a 0 or a 1. That's why the bug takes 8 hits. A screen makes each color by mixing three bytes: how much Red, Green and Blue light to shine.

The idea

What is a bug?

A bug is a mistake in a program. Small bugs grow into big problems when nobody fixes them, just like BUG.EXE grows each time it eats an Octlet.

Programmers call finding and fixing bugs debugging. You do it by testing, watching what goes wrong, and trying again.
